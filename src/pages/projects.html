// 01 Clinical genomics & AI at Lucence

Since November 2025. The assays, data and models are not mine to publish, so these describe the problem shape and the public tools — never samples, results or performance figures.

Population whole-genome sequencing

End-to-end lead for the Singapore nationwide population (PRECISE) WGS workflow: wet-lab library preparation, bioinformatic analysis, reporting, clinical validation and deployment. At population scale a workflow is only as good as its worst week, so library QC — single- versus double-sided size selection at normalised depth — and depth-titration tooling are part of the design.

  • Germline WGS
  • Library prep
  • QC
  • Clinical validation

cfDNA MRD and multi-cancer early detection

Wet-lab and pipeline development for minimal residual disease and MCED from plasma. A low-pass cfDNA CNV consensus (ichorCNA, WisecondorX, BACDAC) estimates tumour fraction, ploidy and LOH at ≤5x coverage, validated with in-silico mixtures of a matched tumour/normal cell-line pair.

  • cfDNA
  • Low-pass WGS
  • Tumour fraction
  • Fragmentomics

Somatic FFPE-WGS and the CNV/SV stack

A modular pipeline — fastp → BWA-MEM → GATK MarkDuplicates/BQSR → DeepVariant / Mutect2 / DeepSomatic → CNVkit / ichorCNA — that detects existing BAMs and makes BQSR configurable. Germline and somatic CNV/SV calling (GATK gCNV, CNVkit, Control-FREEC, Manta, DELLY, PureCN, ascatNGS; merged with SURVIVOR/Truvari) feeds germline calls as priors into the FFPE and cfDNA pipelines.

  • FFPE
  • Somatic calling
  • CNV / SV
  • GATK

Benchmarking against truth

Every caller is measured, not trusted: GIAB HG002 (v4.2.1, CMRG) with hap.py, vcfeval and Truvari, plus a curated panel of about twenty cancer cell lines and reference samples with tiered somatic truth sets drawn from SEQC2/GIAB and DepMap/CCLE.

  • GIAB
  • SEQC2
  • hap.py
  • Truvari

ML for tumour classification

Models that separate cancer signal from noise in sequencing-derived features, built for a diagnostics setting rather than a leaderboard. Calibration, batch robustness and interpretability matter more than the headline metric, because the cost of being wrong is a patient rather than a rank.

  • Machine learning
  • Classification
  • Calibration
  • AI for LS

Nanobody–NGS multi-omics assay

Developed and clinically validated a nanobody–NGS-based multi-omics detection assay. In multi-omics the interesting part is rarely the model — it is deciding which layers carry independent signal and which re-describe the same thing.

  • Multi-omics
  • Assay development
  • NGS

Optimised mRNA and siRNA design, chemical modification, synthesis and purification, including mRNA–LNP formulation. Designed ligand-mediated oligonucleotide delivery and validated its biodistribution in murine tumour models; explored a cell-penetrating-peptide platform for delivery, vaccines and TCR-T.

Dendritic-cell cancer vaccines

Peptide-based antigen–adjuvant platforms for monocyte-derived dendritic-cell (Mo-DC) vaccines, building on the lab’s finding that a nucleolar GAR/RGG motif behaves as an alarmin (Cell Death Dis, 2021) — which I presented as an oral talk at the 16th International Symposium on Dendritic Cells (2022).

TCR-T cell therapy

// 03 AI for Life Science builds

Built on evenings, to learn the field properly by making it runnable. Every lab is real code in pure numpy, and every test suite runs with the network unplugged.

Private repositories. Educational and research use only — not medical advice and not for clinical decisions.

AI-for-Life-Science atlas and curriculum

AI for LS

A map of the AI reshaping biology and medicine — about a hundred tools and models, 43 landmark papers, 24 algorithm cards and 20 datasets and benchmarks — where every row carries an evidence grade: peer-reviewed, preprint, self-reported or unverified. The grade is the point: it separates what was shown from what was announced.

It teaches the field from zero across twelve levels: computing → biology → statistics → ML → deep learning → genomics → single-cell and spatial → proteins → proteomics → multi-omics → medicine → drug discovery. Twenty labs include sequence alignment, FDR, data leakage, a trained 1D-CNN, in-silico mutagenesis, a single-cell pipeline, co-evolution contacts and survival analysis.

// 04 Engineering practice

Five more systems I built and still run, outside life science on purpose: they are where I practise engineering without a deadline. They share one architecture: pure, injectable domain logic with no UI imports; a single layer allowed to touch the network; every network call cached and degrading to stale rather than crashing; and a test suite that runs with the network unplugged.

These repositories are private and stay private — they hold a real household's financial records. What follows describes the engineering only. Nothing here is financial advice, and none of it trades real money.

Quantum-inspired portfolio selection

optimization

Picking the best K stocks out of N is a combinatorial problem — thirty candidates and ten slots is about thirty million subsets, so you cannot simply enumerate them. Posing it as a QUBO turns selection into minimising one quadratic form over binary variables, which a simulated annealer can attack directly. The annealer is 170 lines of numpy: a geometric inverse-temperature schedule, an O(N) incremental energy update per bit flip, and a seeded master/sub RNG so two runs are bit-identical.

Multi-market strategy tournament

research rig

Forty-one strategies race across four markets: rule-based baselines, portfolio constructions on a shared shrunk-covariance scaffold, walk-forward-retrained scikit-learn models, an online multiplicative-weights ensemble, and an LLM allocator. A walk-forward backtest tournament ranks them offline; a live paper A/B runs them all forward in parallel; exactly one champion is promoted by hand.

Multi-currency portfolio dashboard

data modelling

Twelve asset types across several markets, valued in a currency the reader picks at runtime, over a history that must stay comparable across years. The hard part is not the arithmetic — it is keeping two currencies apart: an immutable one that every stored snapshot is denominated in, and a display one that changes whenever someone touches a dropdown. Confuse them and the net-worth chart grows a step change through its middle.

The shape they share

Every one of these repositories converged on one architecture, largely because each rule in it replaced a bug.

The habit underneath is narrower than the diagram: write the check that would have caught it. A colour palette that has to pass a computed colour-blindness check. A test that asserts an optimizer never sees a future bar. A build that fails if a contrast ratio drops below 4.5:1 — including on the page you are reading. None of these are clever. They are just the difference between believing a system works and knowing.
