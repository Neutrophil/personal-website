// 01 What I do

I trained as a chemical engineer, did a PhD in immunology, spent a decade taking therapeutics from the bench towards preclinical studies, and now run clinical genomics from sample to report. The thread is the same throughout: own the whole path, because the decisions made at the bench decide what the algorithm can ever learn.

01 AI for Life Science

Machine learning that survives contact with a clinical assay.

  • Tumour classifiers on sequencing-derived features, built for calibration and batch robustness rather than a leaderboard.
  • Built a 12-level AI-for-Life-Science atlas and curriculum: ~100 tools graded by evidence, 20 labs in pure numpy.
  • Work agent-first with Claude Code, Cowork and agent harnesses — and wrote a 12-module course on running projects that way.
  • Tumour classifiers
  • Python
  • Fragmentomics
  • Agentic workflows

02 Clinical Genomics

Whole-genome sequencing, from a nation's cohort to a drop of plasma.

  • Population-scale germline WGS where library QC — size selection at normalised depth, depth titration — is part of the design.
  • A low-pass cfDNA CNV consensus estimating tumour fraction, ploidy and LOH at ≤5x, for MRD and MCED.
  • Every caller benchmarked against GIAB and SEQC2 truth sets with hap.py, vcfeval and Truvari.
  • Population WGS
  • MRD
  • MCED
  • FFPE-WGS
  • Clinical validation

04 Wet-lab + Bioinformatics

Equally at home running the library prep and writing the variant caller around it.

  • Modular WGS pipeline: fastp → BWA-MEM → GATK → DeepVariant / Mutect2 / DeepSomatic → CNVkit / ichorCNA.
  • Germline and somatic CNV/SV stacks; a low-pass cfDNA CNV consensus for tumour fraction at ≤5x.
  • At the bench: library prep and size selection, qPCR, NGS, FISH, HLA typing, CRISPR, protein expression, flow cytometry.
  • GATK

  • R · Bash · Docker
